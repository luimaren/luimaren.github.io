Luis Martín Encinar

Atomistic simulations · Machine learning · Multiscale modelling

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Nanostructures Physics Group at the Universidad de Valladolid (Spain). I work in computational materials science, combining first-principles calculations, molecular dynamics and machine learning to understand materials at the atomic scale and to make their simulation faster and more predictive.

My current and recent work covers three lines:

  • Machine-learning interatomic potentials for hydrogen storage. Fine-tuning and validating universal MLIPs against DFT for transition-metal-doped carbon materials, and running large-scale molecular dynamics of hydrogen adsorption and desorption.
  • Ge heteroepitaxy on Si. Atom-by-atom simulations of surface diffusion, Si–Ge intermixing, strain relaxation and the nucleation of misfit dislocations during growth.
  • Deep learning for continuum models. Convolutional neural networks that replace costly elasticity solvers in simulations of strained-film morphology, keeping finite-element accuracy over long time scales.

Short bio

I received my PhD in Physics from the Universidad de Valladolid in June 2025, with Cum Laude and International Doctorate mentions. My thesis, Multiscale modelling of Ge heteroepitaxy on Si, was supervised by Luis A. Marqués and Lourdes Pelaz in the Multiscale Materials Modeling group. In 2023 I spent six months as a visiting PhD student at the Università di Milano-Bicocca (Italy) with Francesco Montalenti, developing neural-network models for the growth of strained films.

Before my PhD I graduated in Physics at Valladolid (2018) and completed an MSc in Nuclear Physics at the Universidad Complutense de Madrid (2019), with a master's thesis on energy transport in optimized stellarators supervised at CIEMAT. Since October 2025 I work on hydrogen storage materials with machine-learning interatomic potentials.

I am open to postdoctoral and industrial R&D opportunities in computational materials science and machine learning for materials. Feel free to get in touch.

    Research

    I build simulation workflows that connect scales: first-principles data train fast models, atomistic simulations reveal mechanisms, and machine learning lets larger models reach realistic sizes and times.

    Machine-learning potentials for hydrogen storage

    2025 – now

    Sketch: a carbon layer (grey) doped with transition-metal atoms (amber) that bind hydrogen molecules (blue).

    Storing hydrogen safely and densely is one of the bottlenecks of a hydrogen economy. Carbon materials doped with transition metals can bind hydrogen at useful energies, but simulating adsorption and desorption at realistic temperatures and pressures is out of reach of DFT alone.

    I fine-tune and validate universal machine-learning interatomic potentials for these systems. The work involves generating and curating large DFT and ab initio MD datasets (defective structures, adsorption configurations and out-of-equilibrium geometries), benchmarking frameworks such as MACE, NequIP and UPET against DFT, and running large-scale molecular dynamics of hydrogen uptake and release.

    Related: manuscript in preparation · AI4AM 2026 poster

    Ge heteroepitaxy on Si, atom by atom

    2019 – 2025

    Sketch: a Ge film (amber) on Si (grey). The film has one column less than the substrate, so a misfit dislocation (⊥) relaxes the 4% lattice mismatch.

    Ge and high-Ge SiGe layers grown on Si are key to modern CMOS and silicon photonics, but the 4% lattice mismatch between both materials drives intermixing, surface roughening and, beyond a critical thickness, the formation of misfit dislocations. I used classical molecular dynamics, benchmarked against experiments and ab initio calculations, to follow these processes atom by atom.

    Full-process deposition simulations gave the key growth parameters: intermixing, surface roughness, critical thickness, layer completion and stress relaxation, which I turned into effective parameters for higher-level growth models. Targeted simulations then showed that dislocations nucleate in low-density amorphous pockets below surface valleys, which reorganize into half-loops whose threading arms follow the surface morphology.

    Related: Appl. Surf. Sci. 2025 · Adv. Theory Simul. 2023 · CDE 2023 · CDE 2018

    Deep learning for strained-film growth

    2023 – 2025

    Sketch: a film profile h(x) (bottom) and the surface elastic energy density the network predicts from it (top line).

    Continuum models of strained-film growth need the elastic energy at the film surface at every time step, and computing it with finite elements dominates the cost. During my stay at Milano-Bicocca I designed and trained convolutional neural networks in PyTorch that map a surface profile directly to its elastic energy density.

    We quantified how prediction accuracy depends on the training data and on the features of the profiles, and plugged the network into surface-diffusion simulations of island formation and coarsening, keeping finite-element accuracy over long time scales and large domains. The training datasets are openly available on Materials Cloud.

    Related: Comput. Mater. Sci. 2025 · APL Mach. Learn. 2024 · datasets

    Defects in silicon and SiC

    2021 – 2023

    Sketch: phosphorus atoms (blue) surrounding vacancies (dashed) in the Si lattice.

    Dopant–vacancy complexes control dopant activation in aggressively scaled silicon devices. With first-principles calculations we characterized PnVm clusters through their formation energies, dipole moments and local vibrational modes. Within a project on high-energy irradiation of semiconductors, I also studied the damage generated by low-energy collective collisions in SiC with molecular dynamics.

    Related: CDE 2023 · IBMM 2022 poster
